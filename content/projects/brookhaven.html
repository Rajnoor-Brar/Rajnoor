Abstract

In photoproduction, the electron radiates an almost-real photon that strikes the proton, either as a whole (direct) or through the quarks and gluons it fluctuates into (resolved). Before trusting event generators at the future Electron-Ion Collider (EIC), I tested them where data exist: ZEUS's inclusive-jet measurement at HERA. Pythia 8 was scanned over 96 configurations, Sherpa 3 over 56, at a million events each, and every curve was scored against the data. The scan found the setting that matters most for Pythia, and it showed that Sherpa's samples were too noisy to rank. The EIC Pythia curves so far still run with that setting off. This page says which results stand and which need a rerun. The study runs on Heimdall and was presented at IIT Mandi in November 2026.

The question

At photon virtualities $Q^2 < 1\ \mathrm{GeV}^2$ the exchanged photon is effectively real, and jets from its collisions with the proton probe two structures at once: the proton's parton distributions (PDFs) and the photon's. HERA measured this precisely. The EIC will measure it again at lower energies, $\sqrt{s}$ = 51–141 GeV against HERA's 318 GeV, with eight planned beam settings. A prediction there is only as good as the generator's description of HERA. So the plan is to validate at HERA first, then predict at the EIC.

photo_eic extends the ZEUS analysis to arbitrary beams. Its cuts are analysis options (an inelasticity or $W$ window, the $Q^2$ limit, the jet $E_T$ thresholds, the $\eta$ acceptance and the jet radius), so one plugin serves all eight EIC settings, where before there was one copy per energy. It also books anti-$k_T$ and SISCone jets and charged-particle spectra.

Validating Pythia 8 against ZEUS

The Pythia scan crossed four proton PDFs, the multiparton-interaction scale $p_{T0}^{\mathrm{ref}}$ from 3.0 to 4.0 GeV, and two switches on the lepton: its QED shower and its PDF. That makes 96 points, each compared with ZEUS on 12 distributions, 288 comparisons in all. Every comparison was scored on several metrics (statistical compatibility, normalisation, shape, trend, local defects), combined into one loss per point. Points were ranked by that loss, and each setting was judged by its marginal: how a point with that value does relative to otherwise identical points.

The lepton-PDF result has a clean mechanism. In Pythia, the photon for direct processes comes through the lepton's PDF. Switch it off and the direct contribution is exactly zero: the predicted yield falls from 1.13 to 0.80 of the data, and the spectrum drifts lower with $E_T$, where direct processes weigh more.

Sherpa: why its rankings do not count

Sherpa 3 was run at next-to-leading order with weighted events, across eight PDFs and the $\Delta R$ of its generation-level jet finder. The weights were so uneven that a million events carried the statistical power of about 22. Bin errors were about 16 % of the yield, against under 1 % for Pythia. Loss differences between neighbouring points were smaller than that noise, so a rerun would reorder them.

Not a finding

The talk picked $\Delta R = 0.8$ and three PDFs for Sherpa from the marginals. That choice does not survive a closer look. The $\Delta R$ preference is non-monotonic (0.2 good, 0.4 worse, 0.8 best, 1.6 good, 3.2 worse), which is what noise looks like. And $\Delta R$ is a generation-level cut, which should be chosen loose enough not to matter, not ranked against data. Sherpa here also ran only the resolved photon, with no multiparton interactions, so it is not like-for-like with Pythia.

Towards the EIC

The EIC runs so far are Pythia 8 over 11 proton PDFs at all eight beam settings (88 points), and Sherpa 3 over the electron's polarisation (unpolarised, 65, 70, 75 %) at all eight settings. The cross-section falls steeply with collision energy, and the reach in jet $E_T$ shrinks from about 38 GeV at 18 × 275 to about 20 GeV at 5 × 130.

Read with care

These EIC curves were generated with Pythia's lepton PDF off, so they contain no direct photoproduction. At HERA, that is the setting the validation rules out: it removes about 30 % of the cross-section. They show how the resolved part scales with beam energy, not the full EIC prediction. The Sherpa polarisation runs should show no effect at all: a polarised electron on an unpolarised proton, through photon exchange, does not change this cross-section. Their integrated jet yields differ from the unpolarised runs by 10–30 %, more than their quoted errors allow, so they are best read as a sign that the weighted-sample errors are underestimated.

What's next

  • Rerun the EIC Pythia sweep with the configuration the validation chose: lepton PDF on, $p_{T0}^{\mathrm{ref}}$ around 3.2–3.8 GeV.
  • Regenerate Sherpa with unweighted events, add the direct component and multiparton interactions, then repeat the ZEUS comparison before ranking anything.
  • Choose Sherpa's generation-level jet cut by showing the result does not depend on it.
  • Treat polarisation as a null test, and check the bin-error model of weighted samples with a seed sweep.
  • Fix two small bugs: the reversed $\eta$ range in the ZEUS plot legend, and the charged-particle $\eta$ histogram, which is binned in the lab frame while the jet $\eta$ histograms use the ZEUS convention.
